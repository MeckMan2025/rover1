Section 6, Claude Rover's own read (opinions, evidence cited inline)

Heading source I would trust:
 A remounted LSM6DSL gyro (BerryIMU v3) integrated for yaw, with GPS course
 over ground as the absolute reference while moving >0.3 m/s, and the
 magnetometer at most as a slow drift anchor after a fresh on-rover figure-8
 calibration (imu_calibration_tool.py exists in the repo root for this).
 Reasons: (a) the IMU is currently absent so today the only heading is GPS
 course, which is undefined at standstill (cog_acc_deg pegs at 180 indoors)
 and useless during a pivot turn, exactly the maneuver the looping plan adds;
 (b) the effective GNSS solution rate is 1 Hz (measured, section 4), so
 course-only heading reacts slowly at 0.25 m/s; (c) the magnetometer sits
 near four brushed motors and their wiring, and no calibration was ever saved.

Why the July follower never got its real field run:
 It was started outdoors, briefly. HTTP logs (05b) show three starts from the
 phone on Jul 19 17:57 to 18:00, each stopped manually within 11 to 32 s, and
 one more bench start at 18:17. The RTK log that evening (04c) shows FIXED
 arriving only in 1 to 17 s bursts. My read: the hAcc<=0.5 m entry gate plus
 the aligning phase needing a confident course meant the mission likely sat
 in acquiring or aligning during those short windows and the operator stopped
 it; then the project went dormant (zero /api/nav traffic since Jul 19).
 Nothing in the journal proves wheels ever turned; nav.py phase transitions
 are not logged to stdout, which itself is worth fixing before the next try.

Top three risks for a hands-off sidewalk 1->4->1 x3 run:
 1. Heading loss at the turnarounds. With no gyro, every 180 pivot ends with
    unknown heading until the rover has crept forward far enough for GPS
    course to settle (1 Hz updates). A 70 deg initial error at 0.25 m/s can
    eat the 2 m cross-track abort budget before the controller sees it.
    Remounting the IMU is the single highest-leverage fix.
 2. RTK dropouts and the pause behavior. The only outdoor dataset we have
    (Jul 19) shows 10 FIXED stretches totaling 49 s over ~45 min, FLOAT the
    rest. Expect frequent pause-on-loss stalls mid-sidewalk; a 3-loop
    mission will probably exceed the 300 s mission cap while paused unless
    the plan retunes caps for FLOAT-grade accuracy or gates on hAcc not
    carrier status.
 3. Operator-out-of-loop safety. No physical kill switch is documented
    anywhere I can find; the known stale-WebSocket backlog failure mode has
    previously delayed phone commands (including stop) by 5+ s; and the Pi
    was soft-throttling at 84.5 C today under camera + H.264 + kiosk load
    (vcgencmd throttled=0xf0008, and the 0xf0000 bits say under-voltage and
    throttling have occurred this boot). Mitigations before the run: inline
    12 V motor-supply switch, kill the Halloween kiosk load, and have the
    mission watchdog fail safe on telemetry silence.
